GEIKF and Unified Online Estimation of State and Covariance Parameters

Generalized Extended Information Kalman Filter (GEIKF) for State‑Dependent Covariances

Introduction

The Information Kalman Filter (IKF) is algebraically equivalent to the standard Kalman filter (KF) but offers several advantages in certain applications:

  • Distributed filtering: Information contributions from multiple sensors can be fused by simple addition.
  • Numerical stability: The information matrix update is additive, avoiding the subtraction that can lead to loss of positive definiteness in the covariance update.
  • Initialisation with no prior: Setting (infinite covariance) represents complete ignorance, which is not possible in the covariance form.

This chapter presents the Generalized Extended Information Kalman Filter (GEIKF) for systems where the process noise covariance and the measurement noise covariance depend on the state (or on unknown parameters ). The GEIKF includes the correction terms and that arise from linearising and .

Foundational references on information filtering include Maybeck (1979) and Bar‑Shalom et al. (2001). The extension to state‑dependent noise (the GEIKF) is discussed in the context of the Extended Information Filter (EIF) in modern estimation textbooks. Specific references for the GEIKF (or closely related algorithms) include:

Reference [2]: Särkkä, S. (2013). Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing. Cambridge University Press. (Chapter 6 discusses the extended information filter; exercises mention state‑dependent noise.)

Reference [3]: Crassidis, J. L., & Junkins, J. L. (2011). Optimal Estimation of Dynamic Systems, 2nd ed. CRC Press. (Discusses the information filter and its extensions.)

7. Conclusions and Takeaways

We have presented a unified information‑form framework for online joint estimation of state and parameters that define the noise covariances and . Starting from the maximum likelihood principle for an augmented state with state‑dependent covariances, we derived:

  1. Generalized Extended Information Kalman Filter (GEIKF) – the information‑form equivalent of the GEKF, including the correction terms and .
  2. Dual information filter – obtained by setting the cross‑information , leading to a decoupled state filter and a separate parameter filter that uses the sensitivity .
  3. Recursive maximum likelihood (RML) in information form – a further simplification where the parameter information matrix is ignored and the update is replaced by a step‑size (constant or natural gradient).

Key unifying insights:

  • All methods use the same innovation ; no artificial measurement is required.
  • The information form is particularly convenient for the measurement update, which is purely additive and numerically stable.
  • The dependence of and on introduces additional terms () in the prediction and measurement information contributions.
  • Observability of can be assessed by monitoring the information matrix.

Practical recommendations:

  • Use the full augmented GEIKF when is small and maximum accuracy is needed.
  • Use the dual information filter for moderate dimensions; the additive update for (in information form) is elegant.
  • Use RML with a carefully tuned step size for large or real‑time constraints. The information form offers no particular advantage for RML.
