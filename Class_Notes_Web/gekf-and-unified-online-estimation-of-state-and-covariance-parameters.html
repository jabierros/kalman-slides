GEKF and Unified Online Estimation of State and Covariance Parameters

Unified Online Estimation of State and Covariance Parameters

Introduction

The Kalman filter (KF) provides optimal state estimates for linear Gaussian systems when the process noise covariance and measurement noise covariance are exactly known. In many practical applications—adaptive navigation, target tracking, signal processing, and control—these covariances are unknown or change over time. Online estimation of and together with the state is essential for robust performance.

Existing approaches include the dual Kalman filter (DKF) and recursive maximum likelihood (RML). These are often presented as standalone methods with heuristic justifications (e.g., treating the innovation as a pseudo‑measurement for the parameters). This chapter offers a unified derivation starting from first principles: the maximum likelihood (ML) principle applied to the augmented state that includes both the original state and the unknown parameters defining and . The key novelty is that we explicitly allow and to depend on the state (here, on ), which introduces additional terms in the filter recursions.

The filter that includes the correction terms and (derived from linearising and ) is known in the literature as the Generalized Extended Kalman Filter (GEKF) for state‑dependent noise. Foundational references include Maybeck (1979) and Bar‑Shalom et al. (2001), and more recent works such as Aravkin & Burke (2012) and the 2024 IEEE/CAA Journal of Automatica Sinica paper on adaptive Kalman filtering. The mathematical results presented here are not claimed as novel; rather, the contribution of this chapter lies in the pedagogical framing and structural clarity: we systematically derive the GEKF from the likelihood, then show how the dual Kalman filter and RML arise as natural simplifications of the same underlying framework. This unified perspective, which emphasises the single innovation and the decoupling hypotheses, is the primary educational value offered here.

From this unified perspective, we show:

  • The full augmented extended Kalman filter (EKF) that optimally (under linearisation) estimates and simultaneously.
  • The dual Kalman filter emerges as a simplification obtained by setting the cross‑covariance between and to zero.
  • Recursive maximum likelihood appears as a further simplification where the parameter covariance is ignored and the Kalman gain is replaced by a step‑size (scalar or matrix).

All methods use the same innovation ; no artificial measurement equation is needed. The chapter also discusses observability in the presence of state‑dependent covariances and provides practical recommendations.

7. Conclusions and Takeaways

We have presented a unified framework for online joint estimation of state and parameters that define the noise covariances and . Starting from the maximum likelihood principle for an augmented state with state‑dependent covariances, we derived the full augmented extended Kalman filter that includes the necessary derivative terms (, ) and propagates the cross‑covariance .

From this full filter, two well‑known practical algorithms emerge naturally as simplifications:

  1. Dual Kalman filter – obtained by setting in the state filter, leading to a decoupled state filter and a separate parameter filter that uses the sensitivity . This reduces computational cost and is suitable for moderate‑dimensional problems.

  2. Recursive maximum likelihood – a further simplification where the parameter covariance is ignored and the Kalman gain is replaced by a step size (scalar or natural gradient). This is the lightest method, ideal for high‑dimensional real‑time applications.

Key unifying insights:

  • All methods use the same innovation ; no artificial “zero measurement” is required. The innovation serves as the carrier of information for both state and parameters.
  • The dependence of and on introduces additional terms () in the recursions. These can be included for improved accuracy or neglected for simplicity.
  • Observability of is possible through the covariance of the innovations, even when the measurement mean does not depend on .
  • Extensions for noisy inputs and state‑dependent noise covariances (, ) follow the same linearisation principles.

Practical recommendations:

  • Use the full augmented filter when is small (say ≤10) and maximum accuracy is needed.
  • Use the dual Kalman filter for moderate dimensions (up to a few tens) and when cross‑coupling is important.
  • Use RML with a carefully tuned step size for large or real‑time constraints. For better convergence, consider the natural gradient variant.
