System Observability

Observability is a fundamental concept in control theory and state estimation. It is defined as the ability to determine the state of a system on a given step, given measurements in that and following steps. If a system is observable, it means that by watching its inputs and outputs over a period of time, we can mathematically reconstruct its internal state.

3. Practical Implications and Filter Design

Even if a system is mathematically observable on paper, real-world noise dictates how well we can estimate the state.

The Impact of Noise

  1. Process Noise () reduces information:

    • Larger Faster decay of past information. The filter trusts its internal model less and "forgets" older measurements quicker.

  2. Measurement Noise () limits new information:

    • Note that the effective measurement noise often incorporates both pure sensor noise () and the uncertainty from the inputs mapped through the measurement model ().
    • Larger effective noise Results in a smaller information matrix contribution (). The filter extracts less certain information from incoming sensor data.

Design Guidance

To improve the practical observability of your system, you have two primary avenues:

  • Reduce : Improve your mathematical process models so the system propagates with less uncertainty.
  • Reduce and : Use better, lower-noise sensors and more precise control inputs to capture higher-quality measurements.
