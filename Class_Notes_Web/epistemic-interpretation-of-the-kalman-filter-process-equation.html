2. The Epistemic Shift (Knowledge View)

While the ensemble view provides a physical basis for the distributions, we frequently face scenarios where a physical variable is strictly deterministic (a constant) in reality, but its value is unknown to us.

Instead of inventing separate notations for "true reality" vs. "our knowledge", we undergo an epistemic shift in interpretation while keeping the exact same mathematical variables. The probability distributions no longer strictly need to represent the frequency of outcomes across physical experiments. Instead, the random variables represent our degree of knowledge about the specific physical realization we are currently observing.

If the underlying state is a deterministic constant, its physical variance is zero. However, because we do not know its value, we assign it a pdf (e.g., ). This pdf lives within our algorithm; and are parameters of our belief.

This epistemic paradigm seamlessly adopts the math of the ensemble view. If our assigned statistics perfectly match physical frequencies, the filter tracks the true physical ensemble. If they represent pure belief bounds, the computed distributions seamlessly track the evolution of our knowledge.

4. Conclusion: Reconciling the Views

This reveals the ultimate power of the epistemic formulation: , , and are always treated as random variables in general, regardless of the origin of their randomness. The randomness captured by a variance matrix can be "real" (e.g., physical wind gusts driving ) or "perceived" (e.g., a deterministic temperature measured imperfectly by ). We use the exact same mathematical notation for both. The equations abstract away the origin of the uncertainty, leaving it entirely up to the user to interpret whether the variance represents physical reality or purely a lack of knowledge.

Because we only ever observe one reality, and we observe it through noisy sensors, the ensemble statistics naturally become the exact measure of our epistemic uncertainty. The epistemic view serves as the most general framework: it allows us to interpret our predictions as the absolute best estimates available, whether based on precise physical characterizations or the limits of our current knowledge.
