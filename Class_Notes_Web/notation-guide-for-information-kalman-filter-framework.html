Notation Guide: Information Kalman Filter Framework

This document defines the strict notational conventions used across the Gaussian Information Aggregation framework. The notation is deliberately designed to adhere to an Epistemic View, where the equations track our evolving state of knowledge (belief) rather than just physical mechanics, adhering strictly to a Maximum Likelihood (ML) perspective.

1. The Core Philosophy (The Epistemic View)

In this framework, the primary variables (, , and ) are always treated as random variables, regardless of the origin of their randomness. Their probability density functions (pdfs) and variances represent our degree of knowledge or epistemic uncertainty about the specific physical realization we are observing.

  • Standard math font (): Denotes the epistemic random variable itself.
  • The "Hat" (): Strictly denotes the estimated mean of a knowledge state. Because it is derived from noisy data and uncertain models, any variable with a hat is rigorously treated as a random variable.

4. Information Sources () vs. Estimators ()

To unify the treatment of predictions and observations, any equation that provides mathematical insight into the state is generalized as an "Information Source" ().

We make a strict distinction between the abstract random variable (which retains model noise to facilitate variance/correlation expansion) and its evaluated estimator (which drops the zero-mean noise for numerical computation).
