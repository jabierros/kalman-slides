Notation Guide: Information Kalman Filter Framework

This document defines the strict notational conventions used across the Gaussian Information Aggregation framework. The notation is deliberately designed to adhere to an Epistemic View, strictly separating physical reality from our evolving state of knowledge (belief).

1. The Core Philosophy (The Tri-Layer Epistemic View)

In this framework, we maintain a rigorous boundary between the true deterministic system and our mathematical tools for inference. Every system magnitude exists across up to three conceptual layers:

  1. Standard math font (): Denotes the deterministic physical reality. These are the exact, true values of the system. There is no variance in these variables.
  2. The "Meas" Superscript (): Denotes the logged data realization. These are the fixed, deterministic numerical arrays returned by our hardware (measuring instruments).
  3. The Epistemic Superscript (): Denotes the epistemic random variable. This represents our degree of knowledge or belief about the exact deterministic reality. Their probability density functions and variances capture our ignorance.

The "Hat" (): Strictly denotes the estimated mean of a knowledge state. Because it is derived from noisy data and uncertain models, it parameterizes our epistemic distributions.

4. Information Sources () vs. Estimators ()

To unify the treatment of predictions and observations, any equation that provides mathematical insight into the true state is generalized as an "Information Source".

We make a strict distinction between the abstract epistemic random variable (which retains model noise and hardware variances to facilitate correlation expansion) and its evaluated estimator (which drops the zero-mean noise for numerical computation).

Superscript Conventions for Prediction, Observation, Backward, Smoothed, and Forward Quantities

  • Prediction (pred): a priori estimate before incorporating a measurement at the same time step.
  • Observation (obs): information derived directly from a sensor measurement at time .
  • Backward (bck): used in smoothing (e.g., Two‑Filter Smoother) to denote information obtained from future measurements.
  • Smoothed (sm): the final fused estimate after combining forward and backward information.
  • Forward (fwd): information from the forward pass after the update step (the posterior at time ). This is equivalent to the aggregated in the forward filter, but is explicitly labelled to distinguish it from backward or smoothed quantities when both passes are present.
  • Combined superscripts (e.g., pred,bck): prediction in the backward pass.

Ambiguity note: When we need to distinguish between a temporal sequence of scalars and a subset of indices inside a single vector, we separate the two with a comma: the first index range refers to vector components (row), the second to time (column).
